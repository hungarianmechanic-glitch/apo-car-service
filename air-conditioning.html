Your car’s air conditioning has a job to do all year. Here is why regular checks, the correct refrigerant charge and careful leak detection matter.

A working system can still lose refrigerant

A car’s air conditioning is a closed circuit, but small amounts of refrigerant can gradually escape through hoses and seals. Depending on its design, an otherwise working system can lose up to 10% of its refrigerant in a year.

500 g → 50 g

For a system with a 500 g charge, a 10% loss would be 50 g over a year. This is an illustration, not a fixed annual loss for every car.

Some loss does not automatically mean a component has failed. However, a shortage can build up over time and reduce cooling performance. When refrigerant is missing, I check whether there is a leak that needs repair before recharging.

Technical reference: HELLA air conditioning technical guide.

Pressure alone cannot tell us how much is left

Pressure readings help diagnose how the system is working, but they cannot accurately establish the mass of refrigerant inside it.

To check the charge quantity, the full recoverable refrigerant charge must be removed using a service machine and weighed. The recovered amount can then be compared with the vehicle specification. After the necessary checks, the system is charged by weight to the manufacturer’s specified quantity.

Why vacuum drying matters

Before recharging, the system is evacuated. This removes air and helps extract moisture from the refrigerant circuit. At low pressure, moisture evaporates more readily, allowing the vacuum pump to remove it.

Moisture left inside can interfere with operation and contribute to internal damage. Proper evacuation is an important part of a careful service, rather than a step to rush through.

A vacuum-hold check can reveal a problem, but it does not identify the exact leak location or rule out every leak under pressure.

Technical reference: MAHLE A/C Check.

Forming-gas testing: finding where it escapes

When a leak is suspected, finding its source matters. Forming-gas testing uses a mixture of 95% nitrogen and 5% hydrogen in the empty system. A dedicated electronic detector senses escaping hydrogen and helps locate the leak.

This allows leak investigation without filling the system with refrigerant first, and without adding UV dye. Testing under pressure also complements a vacuum test, helping establish what needs repairing before the final recharge.

Technical reference: HELLA: air conditioning leak detection.

Equipment in my workshop

Precision with TEXA 780 Dual Gas

I use a TEXA 780 Dual Gas service station for vehicles using R134a or R1234yf refrigerant. It handles the two refrigerants separately and supports controlled recovery, evacuation and recharging.

Modern systems need the specified charge within the manufacturer’s tolerance. With a smaller total charge, a few tens of grams represent a larger proportion of the refrigerant. Accurate weighing and controlled filling are essential.

The machine’s precision, combined with the correct procedure and vehicle data, helps me return the system to its specified refrigerant quantity.

Equipment reference: TEXA KONFORT range.

Why use A/C in winter?

Wet coats, damp shoes and cold glass are a familiar combination on an Irish winter morning. Moisture builds up in the cabin and condenses on the windows, reducing visibility.

Air conditioning removes moisture from the air. Used together with the heater, it helps clear misted windows while keeping the cabin warm. Letting fresh air into the cabin also helps when demisting.

In very cold conditions, some vehicles limit compressor operation automatically. Follow your car’s handbook and use its demisting setting. Always clear the windows properly before driving.

Technical references: Ford: climate control and demisting; Ford: temperature-dependent operation.

How often should it be checked?

An annual operating check is a sensible part of looking after your car. Cabin-filter replacement and cleaning should follow the vehicle’s requirements and how it is used.

An annual check does not automatically mean a recharge every year. The manufacturer’s instructions and the condition of the system determine the work needed. If cooling becomes weak, an unpleasant smell develops or you hear an unusual noise, have it checked rather than waiting for the next hot day.

Technical reference: DENSO: year-round operation and annual maintenance.
